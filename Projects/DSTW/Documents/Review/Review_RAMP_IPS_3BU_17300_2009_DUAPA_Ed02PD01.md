# Review – RAMP_IPS_3BU_17300_2009_DUAPA_Ed02PD01

**Document under review:** [RAMP_IPS_3BU_17300_2009_DUAPA_Ed02PD01.docx](./RAMP_IPS_3BU_17300_2009_DUAPA_Ed02PD01.docx)  
**Scope of review:** Chapter 3, “Description of the system,” including the system overview, hardware components and RAM design rules.  
**Conclusion:** The IPS role as the infrastructure platform for the DIL-CU, including VIL and ADM services, is consistent with the architecture shown. However, Chapter 3 states a capacity of 435 interlockings, whereas the available IPS and GA SEMPs state up to 287. It also presents geo-redundancy and uninterrupted operation following an Availability Zone (AZ) failure as current capabilities without sufficiently distinguishing the applicable release or providing the supporting failure/capacity assumptions. Blanket statements about all hardware being redundant and hot-swappable should be tied to the approved hardware configuration. These Chapter 3 statements are also present in the previous available IPS RAM Plan edition; the current edition’s change history records reference updates, so they do not appear to be newly introduced.

The proposed wording below is written as document-ready prose. Confirm the capacity, release scope and hardware configuration against the approved project baselines before issuing the plan.

## Findings and recommended actions

| Topic | Finding | Recommended action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3.1 IPS capacity | The System Overview says that IPS is designed to support up to **435 interlockings**. The available IPS SEMP and GA SEMP instead state potential virtualization of up to **287 interlockings** at two to three centralized computing centres, including geo-redundancy. | Reconcile the capacity with the approved customer and system baseline. Use 287 in the RAM Plan if the cited SEMP baseline remains applicable; otherwise update the related SEMPs and RAM Plan consistently to the approved value. |
| 3.1 Geo-redundancy and release scope | The System Overview describes the IPS solution as supporting interlockings across geo-redundant centres. The IPS RAM Plan identifies IPSS OBB 1.0.0, while the available SSS_IPS geo-redundancy requirement EL4-IPS-SSS-REQ-00013 is assigned target release IPSS OBB 2.0.0. The IPS SEMP discusses geo-redundancy at a high level, but this does not establish that all geo-redundancy capabilities belong to the 1.0.0 baseline. | State the deployment scope without attributing features to the wrong release. Confirm which geo-redundancy capabilities are included in the 1.0.0 baseline and which are planned for 2.0.0. |
| 3.2 Availability Zones and failure tolerance | Figure 3 shows three AZs and a core network. The text defines an AZ as typically corresponding to a physical rack, treats it as the largest failure unit per location, and claims that the remaining two AZs preserve sufficient compute and storage for uninterrupted operation after a rack/AZ failure. The cited SEMP and SSS_IPS evidence reviewed does not establish this specific mapping or demonstrate the capacity and service continuity claim. | Describe AZs as logical/infrastructure failure domains unless the approved design confirms a one-to-one rack mapping. Only claim uninterrupted operation after AZ failure where the approved architecture, capacity assessment and system-level verification demonstrate it, including shared network and storage dependencies. |
| 3.2 Hardware redundancy and maintainability | The text says all hardware components are logically and physically redundant, and that servers and network devices have at least two power supplies and fans for hot-pluggable exchange. These are broad, component-specific hardware claims; the reviewed SEMP does not establish them for every component or confirm the selected models and options. | Limit the statement to redundancy and hot-replacement features in the approved hardware baseline and manufacturer configuration. Keep model names as planned only if that status is current; confirm quantities, PSU/fan options, feed separation and replacement constraints against the BOM and equipment data. |
| 3.3 RAM Design Rules | The rules are described as binding architectural conditions that will be developed and mapped to system requirements, while the common definitions and partitioning are delegated to the DIL RAM Plan. The relationship between IPS-specific rules and the shared DIL rules is not explicit. | Clarify that IPS-specific RAM Design Rules are allocated and traced to applicable IPS requirements, and that shared terminology/partitioning follows the DIL RAM Plan. Avoid implying approved/binding rules until they are baselined. |

## Limitations and open confirmations

1. The two available SEMP sources agree on 287 interlockings, but the customer/contract baseline was not independently verified. Confirm the applicable capacity before baselining the proposed wording.
2. The IPS SEMP describes geo-redundancy at a high level, while the available IPS SSS assigns a geo-redundancy requirement to IPSS OBB 2.0.0. Confirm the actual geo-redundancy scope of IPSS OBB 1.0.0 before describing it as an implemented capability.
3. Figure 3 shows a three-AZ arrangement with a core network, but the reviewed documents do not demonstrate the stated no-impact response to loss of an AZ or confirm that each AZ is exactly one physical rack. Verify these details against the approved architecture and availability analysis.
4. The selected server/router models, dual PSU/fan options and hot-replacement characteristics require confirmation against the approved BOM, configuration and manufacturer documentation.
